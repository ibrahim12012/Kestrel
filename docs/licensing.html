2. The distinction that catches people out

The GPL binds you when you convey the software to someone else, not when you merely run it. Using Kestrel inside your company, unmodified and undistributed, does not by itself oblige you to publish anything. Shipping a product that contains it does.

If you modify Kestrel and distribute your version, the GPL v3 requires you to release your changes under the GPL v3 too, and to make the corresponding source available to whoever you gave the binary to.

Anything you produce using Kestrel is yours under either licence. The macros you record, the files it writes for you and the work you get done with it are not derivative works of the program, and nothing here places any claim on them.

Kestrel is built on PyQt6, which carries its own GPL and commercial dual licence. That applies on top of this one. A commercial Kestrel licence does not cover your PyQt6 obligations, and you would need to settle those separately with Riverbank Computing. Third-party licence texts ship in the LICENSES folder in the repository.

6. Model providers

Kestrel does not route your requests through any backend of its own. Chat, autonomous mode and voice call whichever provider you configured directly, using your own API key. Your use of that provider is governed by your agreement with them, not by this licence. A local model involves no third party at all.
