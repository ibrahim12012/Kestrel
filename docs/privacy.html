Privacy

Last updated September 2026

1. There is no Kestrel server

Kestrel has no backend, no account system and no telemetry endpoint of its own. Nothing you type, attach or automate is sent to the people who wrote it. This is not a policy choice that could change quietly; there is no infrastructure on our side for it to be sent to.

2. What stays on your machine

  • Your macro library, both literal recordings and generalized macros, as plain JSON files in your user data folder.
  • Your chat history, in a local database.
  • Your assistant profiles and settings.
  • Files Kestrel generates for you, in the folder you chose.
  • API keys, in the Windows Credential Manager rather than a plaintext file.

Uninstalling Kestrel or deleting its data folder removes all of it. There is no server-side copy.

3. What goes to your model provider

Chat messages, autonomous-mode requests, attached file contents and the screenshots autonomous mode takes are sent directly to whichever provider you configured, using your own API key, under your own agreement with them. Screenshots are used to work out the next action for that one step.

If you point Kestrel at a local model, none of this leaves your machine.

4. Voice is the exception

Speech recognition runs through Google's free Web Speech API. Spoken input therefore leaves your machine even when your model is local. Voice is off until you turn it on, and the wake word is off by default on top of that.

5. What Kestrel does not do

  • It does not continuously record your screen. Screenshots are taken during an active autonomous run, or when you press the screenshot shortcut, and at no other time.
  • It does not log keystrokes outside its own chat input and the recorder while recording is running.
  • It does not read files you have not pointed it at.
  • It does not phone home.

6. Changes

If this page changes materially, the date at the top changes with it. The source is public, so you can also verify any of the above yourself rather than taking it on trust.
